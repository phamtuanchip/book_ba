# From Zero to IT Business Analyst: A Free Vietnamese Book With Filled-In Document Templates

*40 chapters and 6 appendices covering SDLC, Agile, requirements, BRD/SRS/user stories, and a full worked example.*

"What does a Business Analyst actually do?" is one of the hardest questions in IT. The role sits between the business and the engineers, and most learning material either stays theoretical or assumes you already work in a software team.

I wrote a free book that takes someone with **no BA background** to job-ready: the process, the thinking, and — most importantly — the documents. **It is written in Vietnamese today; an English edition is planned.**

## Who it is for

- Career changers and fresh graduates who want to become an IT BA
- Developers, testers or support staff moving toward analysis
- Junior BAs who want reference templates and a structured path

## What is inside

The book has seven parts and follows one running example project, **FoodNow** (a food-delivery platform):

- **Part 0 — Orientation:** who a BA is, SDLC overview, the project ecosystem, career paths and certifications (IIBA CBAP/CCBA, PMI-PBA), skills and tools
- **Part 1 — Development processes:** Waterfall, Agile principles, Scrum, XP, Kanban, and the BA's exact role in each model
- **Part 2 — Requirements:** elicitation techniques, stakeholder analysis and RACI, requirement types, use case/activity/sequence/BPMN modelling, traceability matrix, validation, change management
- **Part 3 — Core BA documents:** BRD vs PRD, SRS following IEEE 830, FRD and non-functional requirements, use case specifications, user stories with acceptance criteria (INVEST, Gherkin), epics and backlog decomposition, wireframes
- **Part 4 — Delivery:** backlog prioritisation (MoSCoW, WSJF, Kano), sprint planning, incremental delivery, release plans, estimation, RAID log
- **Part 5 — Communication and acceptance:** stakeholder communication, workshop facilitation, working with QA, UAT, sign-off, measuring success after go-live
- **Part 6 — Advanced skills:** Jira and Confluence, SQL basics for BAs, domain knowledge (banking, e-commerce, insurance), and a full FoodNow case study

## The part I am proudest of: filled-in templates

Most books give you a blank template. This one gives you **complete examples**:

- A full BRD/PRD
- A full SRS following IEEE 830
- An epic and user-story backlog
- An incremental delivery plan
- A BA glossary for quick lookup

Read them to learn what "good" looks like, then copy the structure into your own project.
